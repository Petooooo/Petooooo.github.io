## Why a GPU request is a scheduling contract

Kubernetes schedules using resources advertised by the node and its device plugins. The scheduler does not inherently understand every accelerator's physical sharing constraints. This matters when a model needs a full accelerator, a particular memory capacity or predictable performance.

I considered several ways to balance utilization against workload isolation.

| Approach | Benefit | Limitation to evaluate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-device placement | Simple resource ownership and predictable isolation | Reduced packing flexibility and higher idle capacity |
| Device-plugin time-slicing | More logical slots for compatible workloads | Does not itself provide independent physical GPU memory or fault isolation |
| MIG, where supported | Stronger hardware partitioning model | Device and workload compatibility requirements |
| Replica placement and rerouting | Service may survive node loss | Lower remaining capacity and possible queueing or latency increases |

The decision is workload-specific. A memory-intensive inference process may be unsuitable for aggressive sharing even if the GPU device plugin can advertise additional logical resources.

## Failure planning: availability versus capacity

I evaluated the distinction between **a service remaining reachable** and **a service retaining full performance after an accelerator or node fails**. For some deployments, a smaller surviving serving pool may be a more credible objective than a claim of seamless full-capacity failover.

Operational questions included: Can the workload be placed on a compatible node? Does the device plugin advertise the expected resource? Is the workload constrained by device memory? Do replicas contend for shared storage or network resources during restart?

## Debugging starts with what the node advertises

GPU scheduling errors can originate at the hardware discovery, node labeling, plugin, scheduler or application layer. I investigated device-plugin and node-feature-discovery behavior as well as placement constraints, without assuming that the presence of GPU hardware guarantees the expected allocatable Kubernetes resource.

## Why it matters

This is another connection between high-level orchestration and the layers beneath it: **memory capacity, device sharing, scheduling and runtime isolation** ultimately define whether a GPU service behaves predictably.
