> **Course-material record (2023–2024).** The official KDT syllabus and archived training notes describe embedded Linux driver exercises. This page records the covered architecture and the design questions I learned; it does not invent a completed personal driver repository or test measurement.

## Why the device driver is a distinct boundary

Applications interact with hardware through kernel interfaces and drivers. A device driver must account for **discovery/configuration**, **data transfer**, **interrupt handling**, and the lifetime of device resources. Unlike user-space code, it may execute in contexts where blocking or heavy work is prohibited.

The curriculum covered ARM and Raspberry Pi environments, kernel configuration and cross-compilation, as well as character/platform driver concepts.

| Topic | Role in the design |
| --- | --- |
| Boot process and device tree | Describing hardware and associating devices with drivers |
| Character and platform drivers | Exposing hardware operations through kernel frameworks |
| GPIO / SPI / PWM | Communicating with peripherals at different abstraction levels |
| IRQ and threaded IRQ | Responding to events while respecting interrupt-context constraints |
| Workqueues and timers | Moving slower work out of time-critical execution contexts |

## What I took away

The most valuable lesson was learning to reason about **execution context**. Code suitable for a normal process may not be safe in a hard interrupt handler. Deferring work, protecting shared state and handling errors are essential parts of a driver design rather than optional polish.

This also helped connect my prior electronic-engineering projects with later Linux systems work: hardware details affect the behavior of software abstractions at every layer.
